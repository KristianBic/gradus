Privacy Policy — Gradus Lift

Last updated: 27 September 2026

The short version

Gradus Lift does not collect anything. What you log stays on your phone, because the app has no way to send it anywhere.

What the app is

A log of the sets you lift — each exercise named by you — and an estimate of your strength drawn from them. It does not give medical or training advice.

What the app stores

Everything you enter — the name of each exercise, every set with its weight, repetitions and time, and your settings — is written to a database file in the app's private storage on your device. Android prevents other apps from reading it.

There is no account, no sign-up and no user identifier of any kind.

What the app sends

Nothing. The released version of Gradus Lift is built without the Android INTERNET permission, which means the operating system will not let it open a network connection even if it tried to. There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no cloud sync.

Permissions

One permission is ever shown to you:

  • Notifications — only if you switch on reminders in Settings. It is requested at that moment, not at first launch, and declining it simply leaves reminders off.

Reminders also bring two permissions Android grants at install time and never asks you about: re-registering the reminders after the phone restarts, and vibrating when one arrives. Neither can read your data or send anything.

There is deliberately no INTERNET permission and no exact-alarm permission. You can check for yourself under Settings → Apps → Lift → Permissions.

When data leaves your phone

Only in these two cases:

  • Export to CSV. Settings → Your data → Export to CSV hands the file to Android's share sheet. Where it goes next — email, a cloud drive, another app — is your choice, and from that point it is governed by whatever service you picked, not by us.
  • Android's own backup. If backup is switched on in your phone's settings, Android may include the app's data in your device backup to your Google account, as it does for most apps. That is Android's feature, under your control, and the backup is not something we can see.

Deleting your data

Settings → Your data → Delete all records permanently erases every exercise and every set. Uninstalling the app removes the database along with it. Neither can be undone, and neither needs our involvement, because we never had a copy.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone.

Changes to this policy

If a future version adds anything that transmits data — cloud backup, for instance — this policy will be updated before that version ships, and the change will be described in the app's release notes.
