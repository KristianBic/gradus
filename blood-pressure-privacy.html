Privacy Policy — Gradus Blood Pressure

Last updated: 27 September 2026

The short version

Gradus Blood Pressure does not collect anything. Your readings never leave your phone, because the app has no way to send them anywhere.

What the app is

A log for the readings you take on your own blood-pressure monitor. It does not measure blood pressure, does not diagnose anything and does not give medical advice.

What the app stores

Everything you enter — your readings (systolic and diastolic pressure, and pulse if you add it), their dates and times, any notes you add, the target you set, and your settings — is written to a database file in the app's private storage on your device. Android prevents other apps from reading it.

Blood-pressure readings are health data. That is exactly why none of it is sent anywhere: there is no server holding a copy that could be breached, sold or handed over, because there is no server.

There is no account, no sign-up and no user identifier of any kind.

What the app sends

Nothing. The released version of Gradus Blood Pressure is built without the Android INTERNET permission, which means the operating system will not let it open a network connection even if it tried to. There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no cloud sync.

Permissions

One permission is ever shown to you:

  • Notifications — only if you switch on the daily reminder in Settings. It is requested at that moment, not at first launch, and declining it simply leaves the reminder off.

The reminder also brings two permissions Android grants at install time and never asks you about: re-registering the reminder after the phone restarts, and vibrating when it arrives. Neither can read your data or send anything.

There is deliberately no INTERNET permission and no exact-alarm permission. You can check for yourself under Settings → Apps → Blood Pressure → Permissions.

When data leaves your phone

Only in these two cases:

  • Export to CSV. Settings → Your data → Export to CSV hands the file to Android's share sheet — usually to send to a doctor. Where it goes next — email, a cloud drive, another app — is your choice, and from that point it is governed by whatever service you picked, not by us.
  • Android's own backup. If backup is switched on in your phone's settings, Android may include the app's data in your device backup to your Google account, as it does for most apps. That is Android's feature, under your control, and the backup is not something we can see.

Deleting your data

Settings → Your data → Delete all records permanently erases every reading. Uninstalling the app removes the database along with it. Neither can be undone, and neither needs our involvement, because we never had a copy.

Children

The app is intended for adults and collects no data from anyone.

Changes to this policy

If a future version adds anything that transmits data — cloud backup, for instance — this policy will be updated before that version ships, and the change will be described in the app's release notes.
